SUNDAY 11/1
Give Up Your Shoulds Day It's a day to stop stressing over something you keep telling yourself you should do, like exercising or getting organized. The laundry has been excused.
SUNDAY 11/1
Zero-Tasking Day Author Nancy Christie came up with it so you'd spend the hour gained when clocks fall back relaxing instead of working. Doing nothing has never sounded so productive.
SUNDAY 11/1
National Cinnamon Day McCormick, the spice company, created it in 2019 to kick off what it calls Cinnamon Season. Somebody in marketing deserves a cinnamon roll.
SUNDAY 11/1
National Author's Day Nellie Verne Burt McPherson, president of a women's club in Bement, Illinois, thought it up in 1928 to thank her favorite author, Irving Bacheller. Fan mail, the original kind.
SUNDAY 11/1
National Calzone Day Calzone is Italian for "trousers," and it began in Naples as a street food for workers on the go. Pizza that came dressed for dinner.
SUNDAY 11/1
Prime Meridian Day In 1884, 25 nations met in Washington, D.C., to pick zero longitude: 22 voted for Greenwich, Haiti voted no, and France and Brazil abstained. Even the map needed a vote.
SUNDAY 11/1
World Vegan Day The word "vegan" was coined in November 1944, and Nov. 1 was picked because nobody knew the exact date. Somebody forgot to write it down.
SUNDAY 11/1
National Deep Fried Clams Day The clams usually deep fried are the soft-shelled kind, nicknamed "clam bellies" or "steamers." Nobody asks which belly they're eating.
MONDAY 11/2
Broadcast Traffic Professionals Day It honors radio and TV traffic departments on the anniversary of the first commercial radio broadcast, KDKA in Pittsburgh in 1920, which reported presidential election results. Thank a traffic director, they make the spots run.
MONDAY 11/2
Cookie Monster Day Cookie Monster's early role on Sesame Street was undefined and he didn't even have a name until the second season. Me glad he got a name.
MONDAY 11/2
National Deviled Egg Day Deviled eggs also go by eggs mimosa, stuffed eggs and dressed eggs. Whatever you call them, they vanish at potlucks.
MONDAY 11/2
Look for Circles Day It's meant for kids learning shapes to look up, down and every which way and point out circles, but adults count too. Your steering wheel is fair game.
TUESDAY 11/3
National Sandwich Day It falls on the birthday of John Montagu, the Earl of Sandwich, who legend says came up with the sandwich in 1762. Best career move: lending his name to lunch.
TUESDAY 11/3
Cliché Day It's a day to use cliché phrases as much as possible, even though they're usually overused. At the end of the day, it is what it is.
TUESDAY 11/3
Give Someone a Dollar Today Day You're supposed to give someone a dollar, whether a friend, a stranger or a charity. A dollar won't buy much, but it buys a smile.
TUESDAY 11/3
World Jellyfish Day Jellyfish are about 95 percent water, have no bones, and aren't even fish. So technically, wobbly water.
WEDNESDAY 11/4
King Tut Day King Tut became pharaoh at nine years old, and his tomb was found by Howard Carter in 1922. Most nine-year-olds just get a bedtime.
WEDNESDAY 11/4
National Candy Day The day says you can finally give in to your sweet tooth and eat as much candy as you want. Official permission to raid the Halloween bucket.
WEDNESDAY 11/4
National Chicken Lady Day It honors Dr. Marthenia "Tina" Dupree, known as "The Chicken Lady," who spent 12 years as Director of Community Relations and Training at one of the world's largest chicken restaurants. A cluck-ing good career.
WEDNESDAY 11/4
National Easy-Bake Oven Day The first Easy-Bake Oven, from Kenner in 1963, baked with an ordinary light bulb. Light bulb moment, indeed.
THURSDAY 11/5
National Doughnut Appreciation Day The first doughnuts in America were little "nuts" of dough, brought over by the Pilgrims, who learned to make them in Holland. Pilgrims came for freedom, stayed for the dough.
THURSDAY 11/5
National Men Make Dinner Day Men who don't usually cook make dinner while everyone else stays out of the kitchen and just eats, stepping in only for an emergency. A smoke detector counts as an emergency.
THURSDAY 11/5
National Love Your Red Hair Day Stephanie and Adrienne Vendetti, founders of the website How to be a Redhead, created it to remind women of every hair color to love who they are. Even brunettes are invited.
THURSDAY 11/5
Bank Transfer Day It began in 2011 after Bank of America announced a $5 monthly debit card fee, and about 40,000 people joined credit unions that day. Five dollars can start a revolution.
THURSDAY 11/5
American Football Day The old American Football League of the 1960s came up with the scoreboard clock, two-point conversions and names on jerseys. Now we know who to cheer for.
FRIDAY 11/6
Basketball Day Basketball began with peach baskets nailed ten feet up at a YMCA gym, nine players per team, and a soccer ball. Nobody was dunking on a peach basket.
FRIDAY 11/6
National Nachos Day Nachos trace to 1943, when the wives of ten U.S. soldiers from Fort Duncan, Texas, stopped at Club Victoria in Piedras Negras, Mexico, where the chef was Ignacio "Nacho." Dinner plans turned into snack history.
FRIDAY 11/6
National Saxophone Day The saxophone's inventor, Adolphe Sax, was born in Belgium in 1814 and played clarinet and flute before moving to Paris in 1842 to start an instrument business. Smooth move, Adolphe.
FRIDAY 11/6
Marooned Without a Compass Day Some folks celebrate by purposely getting lost and finding their way back without a compass. Your phone's map is cheating.
FRIDAY 11/6
Love Your Lawyer Day Created in 2001 by a Fort Lauderdale attorney, it bans lawyer jokes and lawyer bashing for the day. Objection overruled, no jokes today.
SATURDAY 11/7
Hug a Bear Day The teddy bear is named after President Theodore Roosevelt, who was pictured in a famous 1902 cartoon refusing to shoot a bear. Best press a bear ever got.
SATURDAY 11/7
Little League Girls Day In 1973 a New Jersey ruling made it the first state to allow girls in Little League, a year after 11-year-old Marcia Pepe of Hoboken played three games. Play ball, indeed.
SATURDAY 11/7
Book Lovers Day Reading sharpens the mind, reduces stress and makes readers more empathetic. Not bad for something you can do in your pajamas.
SATURDAY 11/7
Notary Public Day The word "notary" comes from "nota," a shorthand developed by Cicero's clerk to take down Cicero's speeches. Some stamps of approval go way back.
